A Request for Quotation asks vendors to price a precisely specified
need. It works when the requirements are known; when they are not, send
a request for information (RFI) first. Everything a vendor needs to
price accurately belongs here: the volumes, and the assumptions behind
them. Nothing internal that a vendor must not see: secrets, other
vendors' prices, budgets.

## 1. Purpose

What is being quoted, in one paragraph.

## 2. Background

What the system does, how the purchased service fits into it, and what
exists today.

## 3. Scope of supply

What is included, and what is explicitly not.

## 4. Requirements

**Mandatory** (pass/fail; a response failing any one is not scored):

| # | Requirement | How the vendor shows it |
|---|---|---|

**Desirable** (scored):

|---|---|---|

## 5. Volumes and assumptions

The quantities to price, with the measurements and assumptions behind
them, so every vendor prices the same thing.

## 6. Acceptance test

How a response's claims will be checked before a decision: trials,
benchmarks, references.

## 7. Pricing format

A table every vendor fills in the same way, including every cost that
applies (minimums, support, overages), and totals for each volume
scenario.

## 8. Evaluation

How responses are compared: the mandatory gate, then weighted criteria.

## 9. Commercial terms

How long the quotation must stay valid; that no commitment exists until
a contract is signed; confidentiality; the contract terms expected (for
example a data processing agreement).

## 10. How to respond

The format, the contents, the address, the deadline.
